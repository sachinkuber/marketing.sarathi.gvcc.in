How the dashboard is designed

This is a tool for someone who checks in for a few minutes a day and has to make safe decisions quickly. The design is calm and plain so that the things that need attention are the only things that stand out.

Colour

A cool light background, white panels, a dark gunmetal menu, and one blue for actions. Red, amber and green are kept for meaning only, so when you see one it is telling you something.

Type

One family, Schibsted Grotesk, in sentence case throughout. No capitals for labels, no monospace, no emoji.

Principles

These shaped the layout of every screen.

Results first

Home opens with results and progress, then what is waiting for you, then problems.

You always know what you are approving

The item is shown as it will appear, with the checks, scores and the exact version fingerprint.

Silence never approves

Late items are reminded, escalated and marked overdue, with a banner and pop-up. They are never approved or published by default.

A button says what it does

Approve, Reject, Reconnect. The same action has the same name everywhere.

Errors give a next step

An empty or failed state says what happened and what to do, without apologising.

Works on a phone

Approvals can be done on a narrow screen. Use the Mockup controls to switch to phone width.

Stop is always in reach

The Stop button sits in the top bar on every screen.

Roles see only their part

Switch role in Mockup controls to see how the menu and screens change.
